Who is Sanrio Grade 6 for?

Sanrio Grade 6 is a writing workshop in the true ELA model. Grade 6 students learn to control tense, write compound and complex sentences, use academic vocabulary and connect ideas, then write multi-paragraph pieces with a thesis and evidence.

  • Grade 6 students who need to write clear, grammatical multi-paragraph pieces.
  • Students who speak English with confidence but whose writing is disconnected, with weak cohesion and little evidence.
  • Students who want an academic-writing base for later Cambridge exams and IELTS.
  • Families who want their child's writing corrected closely in a small class.
Not sure your child is ready?

The placement test includes a short cold write and a quick grammar check. If your child needs more groundwork, we will say so plainly and suggest a better-fitting class.

How is an ELA writing class different from a regular grammar class?

Each cycle follows plan – write – revise – publish. The course opens with a cold write and a quick grammar check to set goals; cycle A writes stories using narrative techniques; cycle B moves from explanation to argument with evidence; publication week selects the best piece, adds peer editing and a public reading. Grammar is taught on the child's own writing (sentence combining and expansion) rather than as disconnected gap-fill exercises.

Who teaches your child?

The program is led by a teacher with a New Jersey (USA) teaching certificate, who teaches to the US InTASC professional standards for teachers — the specific criteria are listed in each section of this page.

HEC has trained foreign and Vietnamese teaching assistants with degrees in English or education, who support small-group discussion, marking and demanding tasks. Research from the Education Endowment Foundation (UK) found that oral language work led by trained teaching assistants is about as effective as teacher-led work.

FAQ

Who is the Sanrio Grade 6 class for?

Grade 6 students who want to write clearly, with structure and evidence in English: controlling tense, using compound and complex sentences, academic vocabulary and cohesion. HEC places students with a free placement test.

What will my child achieve after Sanrio?

At least 85% grammar accuracy in edits; 2–3 paragraphs (180–220 words) with a thesis and support; a 2-minute evidence-based speech.

How is Sanrio different from a regular grammar class?

Students write for real: plan, draft, revise against a rubric and publish, while grammar is taught on their own writing. With no more than 12 students, every piece is read and marked by the teacher.

What comes after Sanrio?

An IELTS foundation (the Sun class when your child is old enough and ready) or an advanced writing seminar. IELTS is not recommended for candidates under 16, so academic writing is an important step in between.
